# Xcrol UX/UI Review — 2026-08-11

**Scope:** Suggestions only — no changes made. Goal per CD: increase first-user sign-ups, improve usability for all users, and grow the site by serving people dissatisfied with mainstream social media — without violating the mission (no ads, no tracking, no algorithms, chronological feeds, friendship-level privacy, 1 post/day at 240 chars, user data ownership, optional invites).

**Method:** Anonymous first-time-visitor walkthrough of the live site (xcrol.com) in a real browser this session, desktop (1200px) and mobile (390px) widths, plus targeted code reads. Evidence: screenshots `xcrol-landing.png`, `xcrol-auth.png`, `xcrol-river-anon.png`, `xcrol-getting-started.png`, `xcrol-tour.png`, `xcrol-town.png`, `xcrol-map.png`, `xcrol-village.png`, `xcrol-tour-overlay.png`, `xcrol-landing-mobile.png` (in `~/Projects/My Agent System/`). Claims about logged-in surfaces are labeled **unverified** where I could not observe them anonymously.

---

## Executive summary — the ten highest-leverage suggestions

1. **Say what Xcrol is, in plain words, on the landing hero.** "Pronounced Scroll. / Your Map of Connection" is evocative but tells a stranger nothing concrete. One sentence like *"A private social network where you decide who sees what — no ads, no tracking, no algorithm"* would do more for sign-ups than anything else on this list.
2. **Rename or subtitle the "USE YOUR POWERS" CTA.** A first-time visitor doesn't know what powers are. "Join Xcrol — it's free" (or "Sign up free") converts strangers; "Use your powers" delights members. Consider showing different CTAs to anon vs. signed-in visitors.
3. **Mobile: the CTA takes ~4 seconds to appear.** *(Corrected 2026-08-12: the original "below the fold" claim was an animation-timing artifact — the screenshot was taken before the intro animation finished. Re-verified at 390×844 post-animation: CTA, taglines, secondary buttons, and stats strip are all above the fold — `xcrol-mobile-postanim.png`.)* The real issue: `Welcome.tsx` gates the CTA behind a 3s GIF phase + 800ms dissolve, so a first-time mobile visitor stares at an animation for ~4s before any button exists. Reducing time-to-CTA (shorter animation, or render the CTA during the animation) is the actual mobile-conversion fix.
4. **Resolve the "invite-only" contradiction.** The shared-post page CTA says "XCROL is an invite-only social platform" (`src/pages/SharedPost.tsx`), while the FAQ says "Do I need an invite to join? No." (`src/pages/getting-started/FaqSection.tsx`). Since sharing was just opened up, the shared-post page is now a primary acquisition surface — its copy telling people they can't join without an invite works directly against sign-ups.
5. **Lead with the Village for social proof, not the stats line.** The landing stats line read "1 moments shared today · 36 hometowns across 14 countries · 2 private streams flowing" (`xcrol-landing.png`). Small "today" numbers signal emptiness (and "1 moments" is a grammar bug). The Village page, by contrast, shows named groups with real member counts (`xcrol-village.png`) — that's the site's best proof of life. Options: drop volatile daily counts and keep only cumulative ones (hometowns/countries), or surface group cards on the landing page.
6. **Label the nav icons.** The header is icon-only (`xcrol-landing.png`, all pages). The metaphor names (River, Village, World, Hearth Surf) are part of Xcrol's charm, but an icon-only row of unfamiliar metaphors is a double veil for newcomers. Text labels under icons (or labels on hover/mobile) would help every user, and ties directly into assessment item #13 (nav breadth / progressive disclosure).
7. **Give unknown URLs a real 404.** `/tour` renders "Profile Not Found — This profile doesn't exist or has been removed" because `/:username` is the catch-all route (`src/App.tsx`, observed live in `xcrol-tour.png`). A dedicated not-found page with links to the River / Getting Started would recover lost visitors instead of confusing them.
8. **Shorten or chapter the tour.** The Pater Familias tour is 15 steps ("1 of 15", `xcrol-tour-overlay.png`). The narrator concept is genuinely distinctive — keep it — but consider a 5-step core tour with optional deep-dive chapters, and let the tour deep-link into the real pages it describes.
9. **Show the product before asking for the sign-up.** The landing page has no screenshot or live glimpse of the River/Village. The anon River teaser ("Showing the 5 most recent public posts. Sign up to see more and to reply", `xcrol-river-anon.png`) is excellent — link to it prominently from the hero ("See what's flowing →").
10. **Make `/install-app` public.** The FAQ tells readers to "Visit /install-app for step-by-step instructions," but the route is wrapped in `ProtectedRoute` (`src/App.tsx`) — anonymous FAQ readers hit the auth wall. PWA installability is a differentiator vs. Big Tech apps; the instructions page can safely be public.

## A. First-visit funnel (sign-ups)

**What works now**
- The personal banner — "I'm CD. I created Xcrol as my private social network. You can use it too. No spying. No ads. Just real people." — is the clearest value statement on the page and the most human thing a refugee from Big Tech can read. It's currently the smallest text on the screen.
- The anon River teaser with real posts, replies, and reactions is the right pattern: show, then gate.
- Invite code being optional (with bonus points if you have one) is the right growth posture — verified on the sign-up form (`xcrol-auth.png`).

**Suggestions**
- **A1. Promote the "No spying. No ads. Just real people." line into the hero** as the subtitle under the logo. It is already CD's voice and already true. The current taglines can stay as flavor below it.
- **A2. Differentiate the page title.** Every page ships `XCROL - Not Being Used to Control You` (observed in tab titles; a few pages like Town and Village set their own). Negative framing works as a manifesto but not as a first impression in a search result or a browser tab. Consider `XCROL — a private social network you control` for the landing/default title, keeping the manifesto line in the hero or About. Per-page titles (River, Getting Started, Map…) also help SEO and shared-tab recognition.
- **A3. Fix the stats-line grammar and volatility** ("1 moments shared today"). Pluralization bug aside, any "today" counter will read as zero-ish at current scale. Cumulative counters (hometowns, countries, groups, total posts) only ever grow.
- **A4. Add one visual of the product** — a framed screenshot of the River or the World Map. The World Map illustration (`xcrol-map.png`) is beautiful and unlike anything on mainstream networks; it's arguably the best "what is this place" image the site owns and it's currently hidden behind a nav icon.
- **A5. Shared-post page copy** (see #4 above): change "invite-only social platform" to match reality — e.g., "a private social network built around real friendships. Free to join." The sign-up CTA on that page is otherwise well-placed now that any member can share any post they're entitled to see.
- **A6. The "What makes XCROL different?" button on the shared-post page links to the GoFundMe** (`SharedPost.tsx`). A curious visitor clicking that expects a product explanation and lands on a fundraiser — consider pointing it at `/getting-started` (or a short manifesto page) and putting the GoFundMe link inside that page instead.

## B. Onboarding

- **B1. Sign-up form polish** (`xcrol-auth.png`): the "Display Name" field's placeholder says "username" — but per the FAQ, usernames are immutable while display names are free. If this field actually sets the immutable username, say so on the form ("This becomes your permanent @username — choose carefully"); if it's only the display name, fix the placeholder. Either way the current state invites the most costly mistake a new user can make. *(Which field maps to what is unverified from the outside — worth a check.)*
- **B2. Tell people what happens next.** Nothing on the auth page says "confirm your email, then you land in the River." A one-line "what to expect" under the Create Account button reduces abandonment at the email-confirmation step (a classic drop-off point).
- **B3. Tour**: 15 steps is a commitment (see #8). Also, "Skip forever" is charming but strong — a "Remind me later" middle option keeps the door open. The tour auto-advances (progress bar + pause control) — make sure it also fully respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **B4. First-post nudge.** The 1-post/day Xcrol is the product's core ritual; consider making "write your first Xcrol" the explicit first-run goal after sign-up (empty-state prompt with the 240-char frame explained). *(Post-signup first-run state unverified — I did not create an account.)*

## C. Navigation & information architecture

- **C1. Icon-only header** (see #6). Also note one nav button exposes only an image with alt "Village" and no text (accessibility snapshot of the header); all buttons should have consistent accessible names.
- **C2. The metaphor vocabulary needs a translation layer.** River/Brook/Forest/Village/Town/Castle/Strata/Hearth Surf is a coherent world — a real brand asset — but eight coined terms before a user's first session is a heavy vocabulary load. The World Map page already *is* the translation layer; consider making it the default "what is all this?" destination for new users (e.g., first item in the tour, and linked as "Explore the map" from the hero). A one-line gloss under each label on the map ("The Town — community marketplace") would complete it.
- **C3. Catch-all 404** (see #7). Any mistyped or stale URL currently claims a *profile* doesn't exist, which reads as "the person you're looking for deleted their account."
- **C4. "Back to Powers" on the map page** shows for anonymous visitors and leads to a login-gated page (`xcrol-map.png`, `/powers` is protected per `App.tsx`). For anon visitors it could read "Back to home" and link `/`.
- **C5. Progressive disclosure (assessment #13).** For signed-in newcomers, consider revealing surfaces gradually (River + Village first; Town/Castle/Strata as they level up or via the map) rather than the full nav at once. This is listed as CD-decision item #13 in `docs/ASSESSMENT-2026-08-09.md` — this review's data points (icon-only nav, 8-term vocabulary, 15-step tour) all support doing it.

## D. Content surfaces

- **D1. The River (anon)** — pattern is right. Two additions: a "Sign up to reply" affordance directly on each post (the banner scrolls away), and consider raising the anon teaser from 5 posts to a day's worth — chronological + 1/day means even the full public River is small, and more visible life = more sign-ups. *(Whether "5" is a hard cap or a page size: the banner says 5; adjust as CD prefers.)*
- **D2. The Village** is the strongest social-proof page (named groups, member counts, clear descriptions, visible "Public (Any Xcrol Member)" badges, a Sign up button in context — `xcrol-village.png`). Suggest linking it from the landing hero ("Browse the Village →") and from the getting-started page.
- **D3. The Town** shows a full Craigslist-style category taxonomy (~40 categories, `xcrol-town.png`). At current community size most categories are presumably empty *(unverified — I did not click through)*, which makes the page feel like a ghost town. Options: show only categories with listings + "start the first listing in X" prompts; or collapse to top-level sections until inventory exists.
- **D4. The World Map**: "The Castle — Coming Soon" is shown, while the FAQ describes the Castle as a live feature (points, publishing, 60/40 split). Whichever is true today, make the two agree. *(Castle's actual live state unverified.)*
- **D5. Getting Started page** is genuinely good — concrete numbered steps, per-topic cards, deep links (`xcrol-getting-started.png`). Two additions: put its link in the footer of every public page, and add the FAQ's best differentiators (BYOK Scroll AI, ActivityPub/NOSTR, data export) to a short "Why Xcrol" section near the top — right now the strongest anti-Big-Tech selling points live only deep in the FAQ accordion.

## E. Accessibility & polish

- **E1. The "USE YOUR POWERS" button animates continuously** (scale/glow) — enough that automated clicking flagged it as unstable. Perpetual motion on the primary CTA can also trip vestibular sensitivities; honor `prefers-reduced-motion` and consider animating once on load rather than forever.
- **E2. Label all icon buttons** (C1) and verify focus states along the anon funnel (hero → auth → river). *(Full keyboard/contrast audit not performed — worth a dedicated pass.)*
- **E3. Per-page `<title>`s** (A2) double as an accessibility win for screen-reader and tab navigation.

## F. Growth mechanics (within the mission)

- **F1. The just-shipped open sharing is the growth engine — instrument the funnel it feeds.** Shared link → `/post/:id` → sign-up is now the main organic loop. The suggestions in A5/A6 (copy + destination fixes on that page) are the highest-ROI part of this review because that page is where every share lands.
- **F2. Public-post OG previews.** Shared links currently render the generic site card for scrapers (static `index.html` OG tags), while a public-only `og-post` function exists but is unreferenced. Serving rich previews *for public posts only* (title = author + first line) would make shares dramatically more clickable on Facebook/X/Mastodon without exposing anything non-public — the function's public-only gate already enforces that. This is a suggestion to consider, not a change made; the privacy analysis from the 2026-08-11 sharing work applies.
- **F3. Surface the Fediverse story.** ActivityPub follow-from-Mastodon and NOSTR publishing (per FAQ) are exactly what the target audience (people leaving Big Tech) is looking for, and they're invisible outside the FAQ. A small "Works with the Fediverse" note on the landing page speaks directly to that audience.
- **F4. Invite mechanics visibility.** Invite codes grant bonus points to both parties (FAQ), but nothing on the public site advertises this. A line on the auth page ("Have a friend's invite code? You'll both earn points") makes the existing mechanic do marketing work.
- **F5. `/install-app` public** (see #10) + a small "Install the app" link in the public footer.

# Addendum — deeper pass, 2026-08-11 (same day)

CD asked whether the six open questions could be answered from the product itself. Here is what the evidence says, question by question, plus new findings from the second pass.

## Answers to the six open questions (from evidence, not from CD)

**Q1. Who is the primary target among the dissatisfied?**
The product as built points at a specific person: **someone who left (or wants to leave) Facebook, wants a small trusted circle of real friends and family, and cares about in-person connection — travel, hosting, meetups.** Evidence: Hearth Surf gets a top-nav slot; The World/IRL layer's tour copy is "Real people, in real places. Community begins where you stand"; meetup/hosting requests are gated by friendship level; the Village's most-developed groups are general-interest (Tavern, Travelers Inn, Library); the Town is a local classifieds. The fantasy-RPG register (Pater Familias, quests, NPC, the Castle) speaks most naturally to people who grew up with adventure games and D&D — roughly CD's own GenX cohort — and the friendship-level model mirrors how adults with established offline lives actually think about trust. Two audiences the site *could* serve but currently under-signals: Fediverse/privacy techies (ActivityPub/NOSTR exists but is buried in the FAQ) and writers (Scrolls/Castle exists but is invisible pre-signup). Inference: **primary = Facebook-weary adults who want real-life connection; secondary = writers; tertiary = Fediverse crowd.**

**Q2. Is the sign-up field the immutable username or just display name? — ANSWERED, definitively display name.**
`useAuthPage.ts` sends only `display_name` in the signup metadata; no username is set at registration. The immutable username is claimed later in profile editing (`ProfileEditForm.tsx`: "Usernames cannot be changed once set. Your profile is at xcrol.com/@{username}"), and `ProfileCompleteness.tsx` nudges "Choose a username" as a completeness item. So the earlier concern shrinks to a one-word fix: the Display Name field's placeholder ("username") is simply the wrong word — no costly-mistake risk exists in the flow itself.

**Q3. Is the Castle live or coming soon? — ANSWERED: it's a live antechamber; the interior doesn't exist yet; the revenue split is aspirational.**
- `/the-castle` is a real, working progress page: four tracked criteria (250 points, 10 friends, 3 accepted invites, complete profile) with progress bars (`TheCastle.tsx`).
- At 100% progress the page still says "patience, traveler. The keepers prepare your welcome" — there is no unlocked interior in the code.
- The Castle **Library** is live, public, and functional (publish/unpublish RPCs, view counts, reactions) with exactly one publication as of today: CD's "The First Scroll on Xcrol" (observed live, `xcrol-castle-library2.png`).
- No revenue/60-40/payment code exists anywhere in the frontend, and the stack has no payment integration — the FAQ's "60/40 author/platform revenue split" describes an intention, not a feature.
- So the three surfaces tell three different stories: FAQ says fully live (split included), World Map says "Coming Soon" (`WorldMap.tsx` `disabled: true`), tour says "earned mystery." The tour's framing is the accurate one — suggest aligning the other two to it, and softening the FAQ's revenue-split sentence to future tense until payments exist.

**Q4. Rich OG previews for public posts?**
Can't be answered by evidence alone (it's CD's call), but the evidence shows the *intent already existed*: the `og-post` edge function was purpose-built with a public-only gate and CDN caching — someone designed exactly this feature and it was never wired to scrapers. It is also consistent with both of CD's stated principles this week ("I shouldn't be forcing my point of view on users" + family posts must leak nothing): public posts are, by the author's own choice, public. **Prediction: yes for public posts only.**

**Q5. How married is the site to "Not Being Used to Control You"?**
Git says the title was introduced 2026-07-11 — one month old, a recent deliberate choice, and the README leads with it too. So it likely reflects current conviction, not legacy. It also appears to be the expansion of the name itself (XCROL as manifesto), making it load-bearing brand identity. Revised suggestion accordingly: **keep the phrase, move it.** Use a descriptive `<title>`/og:title ("XCROL — a private social network you control") and let "Not Being Used to Control You" live as the tagline/og:description/manifesto heading — the phrase works better as a battle cry than as a browser-tab label.

**Q6. Growth vs. intimacy — what shape does the product itself want?**
The architecture already answers this: **intimacy at the core, growth at the edges.** The River/Brooks are deliberately capped (tour: "Limits keep the map readable"); meanwhile every scale surface points outward — the Castle *requires 3 accepted invites* (every engaged user becomes a recruiter), invite codes pay points to both parties, the Village holds unlimited parallel groups, the Town is a marketplace taxonomy built for volume, and the IRL map grows by geographic density (stats line counted 42 hometowns / 16 countries on this pass vs 36/14 earlier today — live numbers). The intended shape appears to be: many small trusted circles, federated by groups, marketplace, and place — not one big commons. If that's right, newcomer investment should go to Village + Map + Town surfaces, and the River should be marketed *as* the sanctuary, not the destination.

## New findings from the deeper pass

- **N1. Developers page header bug:** at 1200px the top-right "Manage Apps" and "Sign In" buttons overlap/render on top of each other (`xcrol-developers.png`). The page itself ("Login with Xcrol", endpoint cards, Quick Start) is one of the most polished on the site.
- **N2. Light mode is good — and low-contrast in one spot.** The parchment background suits the brand art arguably better than dark mode (`xcrol-landing-light.png`), but "Your Map of Connection" renders yellow-on-cream (likely below WCAG AA). Worth a contrast pass on light-mode accent text.
- **N3. Castle Library has per-page SEO titles** ("The Castle Library — Published Scrolls | XCROL") — proof the pattern exists in the codebase; extending it site-wide (suggestion A2) is incremental, not new work.
- **N4. Dual naming for friendship levels is inconsistent across surfaces.** In-app labels are "Oath Bound (Close Friends)" style (`friendship-labels.ts` — fantasy name + plain gloss, a good pattern); the FAQ uses fantasy names alone; the README uses plain names alone ("Close Friend / Family / Buddy…"). Suggest one canonical presentation (the in-app "Fantasy (Plain)" pairing) everywhere.
- **N5. Castle Library loads content after a visible spinner delay** on first anonymous visit (`xcrol-castle-library.png` vs `...2.png`) — fine at one publication, worth a skeleton state as the shelf fills.
- **N6. Landing stats are live** (changed between morning and afternoon visits). Reinforces A3: cumulative counters will always read well; the "today" counter is the only fragile one.
